First 90 seconds

Use this sequence with Brian

The objective is not to sound impressive. The objective is to reduce his cognitive load and leave with clean ownership.

Reflect the objective.“My understanding of today’s objective is ______.” Use his language, not your system language.
Give the operational status.“Completed: ____. In progress: ____.” One workstream at a time; no biography or architecture lecture.
Surface the decision—not the anxiety.“Decision needed: ____. My recommendation is ____ because ____.” Bring an answer with the problem.
Close the ownership loop.“I own ____ by ____. ____ owns the dependency by ____. Is that correct?”
Stop.Let Brian clarify, redirect, or approve. Record the correction verbatim before moving on.
Brian’s visible standardTake ownership. Lead yourself. Come with solutions. Convert discussion into an answer, a responsible person, and a next time.
6 AM operator format

Present in the same cadence as the offshore coordination

The observed AEC pattern is brief, workstream-specific, resource-conscious, and closed with a concrete next commitment.

1 · ScopeProject, sheet, view, room, detail, or field location.
2 · StatusCompleted / in progress / blocked. Never “working on it.”
3 · EvidenceDrawing, markup, photo, email, file, measurement, or direct observation.
4 · DependencyExact file, decision, consultant input, owner, or approval needed.
5 · CommitmentRecommended action, named owner, next artifact, and due time.
Meeting disciplineDo not dump a list of discoveries on Brian. Group them by workstream, rank the most consequential decision first, and make the ask binary whenever possible.

Agency guardrails
Protect attention, trust, and scope

        

          
Do not volunteer vague work.Convert every ask into deliverable, acceptance evidence, owner, and due time.

          
Do not explain the whole system.Surface only what moves the current project decision.

          
Do not bring a naked problem.State the issue, your recommended path, and the tradeoff.

          
Do not confuse discussion with authorization.Record proposed work as proposed until Brian assigns it.

          
Do not hide uncertainty.Restate your interpretation and ask one precise confirming question.

          
Do not leave without the loop closed.Name who does what, by when, and what proves completion.
